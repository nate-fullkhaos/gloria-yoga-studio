The Philosophy Behind PsyYogshala

I am Gloria Barbara Menezes, an internationally certified 500-hour Classical Hatha Yoga Teacher from the Patanjali International Yoga Foundation, Rishikesh.

With over 8 years of personal practice and 6 years of teaching experience, yoga has become much more than a profession—it has been my path to finding peace, strength, and balance amidst the chaos of everyday life.

As the founder of PSYYOGSHALA, my vision is to create a space where people don’t just learn yoga, but experience a journey of transformation. My classes focus on mindful movement, breath awareness, alignment, strength, flexibility, and overall well-being, helping each individual build a healthier body and a calmer mind.

The PsyYogshala Journey

Rooted in Awareness, Growing in Balance

Five years ago, a journey to the spiritual heart of Rishikesh sparked a vision. It began as @psyyogi — an exploration of yoga fused with vibrant, electric energy. As years passed and we shared practices that transformed us from the inside out, the meaning behind the name grew deeper. Psyyogi evolved into PsyYogshala. The electric vibe became an inner radiance. Today, PSY stands as a triadic framework of personal transformation: Prāṇa, Śhakti, and Yukti.

P +

Prāṇa (प्राण)

The Soul of the Practice

Click to explore.

The vital life force and breath we cultivate to anchor ourselves completely in the present moment.

Prāṇa represents the unadulterated soul and authentic lineage of classical Yoga. It is the life-force energy that bridges the physical body to consciousness. At PsyYogshala, we ground our practice in the timeless teachings of classical Hatha Yoga and the foundational philosophy of Maharishi Patanjali.

  • Authentic tradition: We honor the classical roots of Hatha Yoga and Raja Yoga, prioritizing internal awareness, breath control, and stillness over superficial trends.
  • Scriptural foundation: Practice is not merely physical effort; it is an inner journey (Antar-Yatra) governed by mindful regulation of vital energy.

Scriptural foundations

Patanjali Yoga Sutras (2.49)

“Tasmin sati śvāsapraśvāsayor gativicchedaḥ prāṇāyāmaḥ”

When stability in posture is achieved, the conscious regulation and transformation of the incoming and outgoing breath begins.

Hatha Yoga Pradipika (2.2)

“Chale vāte chalaṁ chittaṁ nischale nischalaṁ bhavet”

When the breath [Prana] is unstable, the mind is restless; when the breath becomes still, the mind gains steadiness.

S +

Śhakti (शक्ति)

Strength & Dynamic Movement

Click to explore vitality, power, and stamina.

Shakti is the dynamic expression of energy—the power to move, endure, and transform.

If Prana is the soul, Śhakti is the active life force that animates it. It embodies strength, vigor, movement, and physical capability. In our practice, Shakti represents the intentional cultivation of physical fortitude, endurance, and flow.

  • Purposeful movement: Building dynamic core stability, functional strength, and physical endurance.
  • Energy activation: Awakening latent energy (Kundalini / Prana Shakti) to cultivate vitality, resilience, and confidence both on and off the mat.

Scriptural foundations

Śiva Saṁhitā & Classical Tantra

Śhakti is venerated as the cosmic dynamic force (Prakriti)—without which consciousness remains unmanifest. In physical practice, Shakti represents the vessel’s capacity to hold and channel spiritual energy.

Svetasvatara Upanishad (6.8)

“Parasya śaktir vividhaiva śrūyate”

The supreme power of the Divine reveals itself in diverse dynamic forms.

Y +

Yukti (युक्ति)

Intelligent & Modern Synthesis

Click to explore alignment, adaptation, and skill in action.

Yukti is discernment—applying timeless wisdom with modern biomechanical clarity.

Yukti signifies intelligence, skillfulness, and adaptation. We live in a contemporary world with distinct lifestyle challenges—sedentary habits, modern stress, and altered movement patterns. Yukti is our commitment to applying traditional practices through the lens of modern movement science, anatomy, and functional alignment.

  • Smart adaptation: Intelligent sequencing tailored to time, place, individual needs, and modern postural alignment.
  • The bridge: Synthesizing ancient scriptural wisdom with contemporary anatomical understanding to practice safely, sustainably, and effectively.

Scriptural foundations

Bhagavad Gita (2.50)

“Yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam”

Yoga is skill and intelligence in action.

Charaka Samhita (Ayurvedic Canon)

Yukti is defined as the judicious, intelligent application of knowledge according to Kāla (time), Deśa (environment), and Pātra (the individual).

The Approach

How We Guide Your Practice

At PsyYogshala, every session is built around balance — strength and softness, effort and ease.

Every session blends step-by-step asana demonstrations with clear verbal cues so you can follow along seamlessly. We emphasize precise alignment and offer real-time, hands-on or verbal corrections as poses are held—ensuring a safe, mindful experience whether you join us in-person at the studio or online.

You’ll move through structured Hatha-based sequences that focus on alignment, breathwork, and steady progression.

Meet Your Instructor: Behind the Mat with Gloria

Gloria Barbara Menezes

500-Hour RYT · Patanjali International Yoga Foundation

I didn’t find yoga—it found me when I needed it most. In 2020, while working in the pageant, fashion, and sales industries, I signed up for a Yoga Teacher Training in Rishikesh. To be honest, I initially treated it as a joke—a fun excuse to travel to the mountains where yoga was just a side note.

The training hit me with an intensity I never expected. When I nearly gave up during the first few days, I decided to take it on as a personal challenge instead. I committed to showing up on my mat daily, ultimately finishing the course as a completely transformed person—mentally, physically, and spiritually.

Back home in Mangalore, I channeled that raw Rishikesh energy into disciplined self-study. I studied my course material, practiced every asana step-by-step, and recorded daily time-lapses to track my progress. After teaching at a local studio, I returned to Rishikesh to complete my advanced 300-hour training, bringing my total credentials to 500 hours with the Patanjali International Yoga Foundation.

That practice became my anchor through life’s biggest shifts, including marriage and motherhood. I taught and practiced throughout my pregnancy right up to my final week, adapting my movement to support a safe, healthy delivery for both me and my baby.

Today, as a mother and dedicated teacher, I view yoga as a lifelong journey of self-discovery. My goal is to offer authentic, personalized guidance that helps you build confidence, deepen your practice, and reconnect with your true self.

I truly believe that yoga isn’t about touching your toes—it’s about discovering yourself.

“Yoga is not something I teach — it is the way I found my way back to myself. My purpose is to help others discover that same strength, balance, and peace.”
